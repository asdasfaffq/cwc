[1] R2-AVAILABILITY (code). Correct, we thank the reviewer, and it changes no reported number. Proposition 2 already *states* the required policy -- "the fallback plan and the per-cell abstain decision are functions of the selection split alone" -- and the released code deviated from the paper. Auditing the whole flow we found a second deviation no review raised (the fallback was screened over selection+calibration); both are fixed. Re-running under exactly the paper's policy, on identical splits, cells, seeds and Gamma (K in {3,4,6,8,12} x 3 budgets x 5 seeds = 75 blocks): at the headline setting (K=4, 80 ms) the bound is NUMERICALLY IDENTICAL, 0.2629, and realized violation FALLS, 0.0014 -> 0.0011. A union-bound variant keeping the data-dependent rule costs +0.033 of bound width and nothing else. Bound >= realized in 225/225 runs. The defect was in the code, not in the theorem or the conclusions.

[2] R2-W1, R2-D1 (choosing/validating Gamma). Gamma need not be estimated. CWC is transductive, so on any operator-declared coarsening BOTH window marginals are observable and Gamma_c = max_z Q_c(z)/P_c(z) is COMPUTED, not fitted; under within-stratum exchangeability the change-of-measure step of Prop. 2 is unchanged. It is testable and we test it: the train-vs-eval conditional-loss gap per (cell,stratum) is 0.0012 mean, 0.027 max over 15 blocks. Removing the estimate costs 5% of bound width (0.298 -> 0.313). It also shows our plug-in was NOT conservative (it needed kappa*=3.95) -- reported against ourselves. R2-D1 recipe: declare the coarsest partition you accept, compute Gamma from the observed marginals, run the gap test on historical window pairs, refine until it passes. Finer coarsenings raise Gamma monotonically (0.313 -> 0.484 -> 0.570) and return the trivial value 1 for cells whose served stratum is absent from training.

[3] R2-W2, R2-D2 (admission decided before execution). Redone: the DECISION uses only compile-time quantities; only the payoff is settled afterwards. We also removed a confound of our own -- the certificate is a property of the analysis, not of CWC -- by granting it to EVERY deployment, separating rule from policy. 30 blocks, tau=0.05: under the certificate rule all 7 deployments breach 0% of the windows they admit; the strongest certificate-free prospective signal breaches 13.0%, a plain point estimate 33.3%. CWC passes its own certificate on 96.7% of windows against 26.7-33.3% for the calibrated static plans, earning 2.5x their admission utility (0.496 vs 0.200) and more than the CLAIRVOYANT post-hoc rule applied to that baseline (0.400).

[4] R2-W3, R2-D3 (bound tight enough for a real SLA?). R_cert is a floor Gamma*L_hat plus a 1/sqrt(n) term, so an SLA needs two conditions. Sweeping window size: n_c 68 -> 688 drives R_cert 0.368 -> 0.037, valid at every point. tau=0.20 is certified at n_c=121, tau=0.10 at n_c=341, tau=0.05 at n_c=688 (R_cert 0.0435, realized 0.0031). tau=0.01 is sample-size-bound, not floor-bound (floor 0.0015), needing n_c ~ 12k. The 0.30 in the paper is the small 3-collection regime, not a property of the bound.

[5] R1-W2, R2-D4, R3-D2 (end-to-end on a real system). Built and run: sparse-matrix BM25, hnswlib HNSW over E5-base, RRF, a MiniLM cross-encoder, and a metadata predicate placed PRE (constrained ANN traversal, list widened as the predicate tightens) or POST, over a 100,785-doc union of five BEIR collections. Nothing is replayed from cached rows; CWC pays its probe and routing INSIDE the measured path. Pre-filter cost spans 0.48 ms at selectivity 1.0 to 78.6 ms at 0.02, so placement is a real decision. SLO grid fixed a priori as latency quantiles (~10/25/50/100 ms). At the binding SLO, 5 seeds: violations 20.4% -> 0.7% (30x), throughput +75% (27.1 vs 15.4 qps), p95 -28% (82.1 vs 114.4 ms), nDCG cost 3.9% -- matching the ~4% the paper reported from cached rows. All 5/5 seeds favour CWC on violation, p95 and throughput against every baseline (one-sided Wilcoxon, per-comparison p=0.031; at 5 seeds Holm over 4 baselines cannot reach 0.05, so the paper's claim rests on its 45-block study). Open-loop Poisson arrivals into a real queue: CWC's saturation capacity is 21.2 qps vs 18.0 and 12.2, and its response p95 advantage widens with load (175/226, 219/450, 429/1074 ms). Boundary: at the two tightest SLOs one cheap plan is optimal for every cell, so the probe is pure overhead (0.0953 vs 0.0957 nDCG).

[6] R1-W1, R1-RELEVANCE (systems contribution; "Borderline Relevant"). The object is physical-plan selection under a resource constraint. The unit of decision is the workload, as in physical database design, and that choice is what makes the guarantee affordable: because a serving window's cell masses are observed exactly, only K within-cell expectations carry statistical risk, where a per-query certifier pays log M instead of log K (Prop. 3). The mechanism is a compiler decision -- abstain to a feasibility-best fallback in plan space -- not a post-hoc recalibration of a score. The payoff is an SLA admission decision, measured in serving terms in [5]. We claim this design point and the system that makes it operational, not a new retriever or inequality.
