RESPONSE TO REVIEWERS -- Paper 214
(Condensed. Full point-by-point response, all new code, all raw results and the compiled
revision: https://github.com/asdasfaffq/cwc , directory rebuttal/)

We thank all three reviewers. Two comments changed the paper's content, and we lead with them.

(1) R2's code finding is CORRECT. The released compiler let the calibration split both choose
and certify the deployed plan; auditing the rest of the data flow we also found the fallback
was screened on the full training window (selection + calibration). Both are fixed. On 75
blocks (K in {3,4,6,8,12} x 3 budgets x 5 seeds) with identical splits, cells, seeds and
Gamma, the repair that matches Prop. 2 verbatim -- decide abstention on the selection split,
calibrate only the fixed plan -- leaves the certified bound NUMERICALLY IDENTICAL (0.2629 at
K=4/80 ms) and slightly REDUCES realized violation (-0.0006). An alternative repair that keeps
the data-dependent rule and pays a union bound over the two candidates per cell costs +0.033
of bound width and nothing else. The certificate upper-bounds the realized violation in all
225 runs.

(2) R2 is right that Gamma was assumed, not verified. We no longer estimate it. CWC is
transductive, so on any operator-declared coarsening BOTH window marginals are observable and
Gamma_c = max_z Q_c(z)/P_c(z) is COMPUTED, not fitted. The only remaining assumption --
within-stratum exchangeability -- is testable, and we test it: the train-vs-eval conditional
loss gap inside each (cell, stratum) is 0.0012 mean, 0.027 max. Making Gamma auditable costs
5% of bound width (0.298 -> 0.313). It also shows our old plug-in was NOT conservative: it
would have needed kappa* = 3.95 to dominate the measured budget in every cell. We report this
against ourselves.

END-TO-END SYSTEM (R1-W2, R2-D4, R3-D2). New serving harness: real BM25 (sparse term-weight
matrix), hnswlib HNSW over E5-base, RRF, a MiniLM cross-encoder at depth 20/50, and a metadata
predicate placed PRE (constrained ANN traversal, search list widened as the predicate tightens)
or POST, over a 100,785-document union of five BEIR collections. Nothing is replayed from
cached rows: every latency is wall-clock of a real execution, and CWC pays its telemetry probe
and routing INSIDE the measured path. Pre-filter cost spans 0.48 ms at selectivity 1.0 to
78.6 ms at 0.02, so filter placement is a genuine decision. At a 100 ms SLO over 5 seeds, CWC
cuts the realized violation rate of the strongest calibrated static plan by 30x (20.4% ->
0.7%) while delivering 75% more throughput (27.1 vs 15.4 qps) and 28% lower p95 latency
(82.1 vs 114.4 ms), at a 3.9% nDCG cost (0.1013 vs 0.1054) -- within a whisker of the ~4%
the paper reports from cached rows, now obtained by executing every plan. Honest boundary,
which we state rather than average away: at 10-25 ms SLOs a single cheap plan is optimal for
every cell, so CWC's probe is pure overhead (0.0953 vs 0.0957 nDCG at half the throughput).
Open-loop (Poisson arrivals into a queue that executes every request for real, SLO scored on
response time): measured saturation capacity is CWC 21.2 qps vs 18.0 for the strongest
calibrated static plan and 12.2 for the best-quality one, and CWC holds the best tail at every
offered load with the margin widening -- response p95 175 vs 226 ms at 9.05 qps, 219 vs 450 ms
at 13.6 qps, and 429 vs 1074 ms at 18.1 qps (violations 0.696 vs 0.942).

PROSPECTIVE ADMISSION (R2-W2/D2). Redone so the admission DECISION uses only compile-time
information and only the payoff is settled afterwards. We also removed a confound: the
certificate is a property of the analysis, not of CWC, so we grant it to EVERY deployment. On
30 pooled blocks at tau=0.05, under the certificate rule all seven deployments breach 0% of
the windows they admit; the strongest certificate-free prospective signal breaches 13.0% and a
plain point estimate 33.3%. CWC's plan passes its own certificate on 96.7% of windows against
27-33% for the calibrated static plans, so a certified operator serving CWC earns 2.5x the
utility (0.496 vs 0.200) and beats the CLAIRVOYANT post-hoc rule applied to that baseline
(0.400). Against us: on this pooled workload Static-SPLADE is itself safe and scores 0.532.

TIGHTNESS (R2-W3/D3). R_cert has a floor Gamma*L_hat plus a 1/sqrt(n) term, so two separate
conditions govern an SLA level. Sweeping labeled-window size: n_c = 68 -> 688 drives R_cert
0.368 -> 0.037, valid at every point. tau=0.20 is certified at n_c=121, tau=0.10 at n_c=341,
and tau=0.05 at n_c=688 (R_cert 0.0435, realized 0.0031). tau=0.01 is sample-size-bound, not
floor-bound (floor 0.0015), needing n_c ~ 12k. A 5% SLA is therefore certifiable today; the
0.30 in the submission was the small 3-collection regime, not a property of the bound.

MOTIVATION (R3-D1). Measured rather than asserted: as the workload mix drifts, a calibrated
fixed plan's SLO violation rises 0.02% -> 12.4%, its p95 latency triples (3.04x) and its
throughput falls to 37%, while CWC goes 0.15% -> 1.0% with p95 x1.08. The fixed recipes that
stay safe do so by giving up 7-20 nDCG points (Dense-only 0.533, BM25-only 0.407 vs CWC 0.604).
