RESPONSE TO REVIEWERS -- Paper 214
Full point-by-point response, all new code, all raw results and the compiled revision:
https://github.com/asdasfaffq/cwc (directory rebuttal/). Anonymous access re-verified.

We thank the reviewers. Two comments changed the paper's content, and we lead with them.

(1) R2's code finding is CORRECT. The released compiler let the calibration split both choose
and certify the deployed plan. Auditing the rest of the data flow we found a SECOND instance no
review raised -- the fallback was also screened using calibration data -- and we report it
rather than wait for someone else to find it. Both are fixed. On 75 blocks with identical
splits, cells, seeds and Gamma, the repair that matches Prop. 2 verbatim (decide abstention on
the selection split, calibrate only the already-fixed plan) leaves the certified bound
NUMERICALLY IDENTICAL (0.2629 at K=4/80 ms) and LOWERS realized violation (0.0014 -> 0.0011).
An alternative repair that keeps the data-dependent rule and pays a union bound over the two
candidates per cell costs +0.033 of bound width and nothing else. The certificate upper-bounds
the realized violation in 225/225 runs.

(2) R2 is right that Gamma was assumed. We no longer estimate it. CWC is transductive, so on
any operator-declared coarsening BOTH window marginals are observable and
Gamma_c = max_z Q_c(z)/P_c(z) is COMPUTED, not fitted. The only remaining assumption --
within-stratum exchangeability -- is testable, and we test it: the train-vs-eval conditional
loss gap inside each (cell, stratum) is 0.0012 mean, 0.027 max. Auditing Gamma costs 5% of
bound width (0.298 -> 0.313). It also shows our old plug-in was NOT conservative: it would have
needed kappa* = 3.95 to dominate the measured budget. We report that against ourselves.
Operational recipe (R2-D1): declare the coarsest partition on which you accept exchangeability,
compute Gamma from the two observed marginals, run the (cell, stratum) gap test on historical
window pairs, refine until it passes.

END-TO-END SYSTEM (R1-W2, R2-D4, R3-D2). New serving harness, everything real: sparse-matrix
Okapi BM25, hnswlib HNSW over E5-base, RRF, a MiniLM cross-encoder, and a metadata predicate
placed PRE (constrained ANN traversal, search list widened as the predicate tightens) or POST,
over a 100,785-document union of five BEIR collections. No cached rows: every latency is
wall-clock of a real execution, and CWC pays its probe and routing INSIDE the measured path.
Pre-filter cost spans 0.48 ms at selectivity 1.0 to 78.6 ms at 0.02, so placement is a genuine
decision. At a 100 ms SLO over 5 seeds, CWC cuts the realized violation rate of the strongest
calibrated static plan by 30x (20.4% -> 0.7%) with 75% more throughput (27.1 vs 15.4 qps) and
28% lower p95 (82.1 vs 114.4 ms), at a 3.9% nDCG cost -- within a whisker of the ~4% the paper
reports from cached rows, now obtained by executing every plan. Open loop (Poisson arrivals
into a queue that executes every request; SLO scored on response time): CWC's saturation
capacity is 21.2 qps vs 18.0 for the strongest calibrated static plan and 12.2 for the
best-quality one, and its response p95 advantage WIDENS with load -- 175 vs 226 ms, 219 vs
450 ms, 429 vs 1074 ms at the three offered loads. Boundary we state rather than average away:
at 10-25 ms SLOs one cheap plan is optimal for every cell, so CWC's probe is pure overhead
(0.0953 vs 0.0957 nDCG at half the throughput).

PROSPECTIVE ADMISSION (R2-W2/D2). Redone so the DECISION uses only compile-time information. We
also removed a confound in our own first attempt: the certificate is a property of the
analysis, not of CWC, so we grant it to EVERY deployment, separating the rule from the policy
it judges. 30 blocks, tau=0.05: under the certificate rule all seven deployments breach 0% of
the windows they admit; the strongest certificate-free prospective signal breaches 13.0% and a
plain point estimate 33.3%. CWC's plan passes its own certificate on 96.7% of windows vs
26.7-33.3% for the calibrated static plans, so a certified operator serving CWC earns 2.5x the
utility (0.496 vs 0.200) despite the static plan's higher raw nDCG, and more than the
CLAIRVOYANT post-hoc rule applied to it (0.400). Scope, stated plainly: on this pooled
5-collection workload at K=2, Static-SPLADE is itself safe and earns 0.532; that configuration
is outside the paper's evaluation setting, where CWC leads SPLADE (rank 2.51 vs 3.07, nDCG
0.641 vs 0.605).

TIGHTNESS (R2-W3/D3). R_cert carries a floor Gamma*L_hat plus a 1/sqrt(n) term, so an SLA level
needs two conditions. Sweeping window size: n_c = 68 -> 688 drives R_cert 0.368 -> 0.037, valid
throughout. tau=0.20 certified at n_c=121, tau=0.10 at n_c=341, tau=0.05 at n_c=688 (R_cert
0.0435, realized 0.0031). tau=0.01 is sample-size-bound, not floor-bound (floor 0.0015),
needing n_c ~ 12k. A 5% SLA is certifiable today; the 0.30 in the submission was the small
3-collection regime, not a property of the bound.

MOTIVATION (R3-D1). Measured rather than asserted: as the mix drifts, a calibrated fixed plan's
violation rises 0.02% -> 12.4%, its p95 triples (3.04x) and its throughput falls to 37%, while
CWC goes 0.15% -> 1.0% at p95 x1.08. The fixed recipes that stay safe give up 7-20 nDCG points
(Dense-only 0.533, BM25-only 0.407 vs CWC 0.604).

RELEVANCE TO ICDE (R1). The object is physical-plan selection under a resource constraint. The
unit of decision is the workload, as in physical database design, and that choice is what makes
the guarantee affordable: because a serving window's cell masses are observed exactly, only K
within-cell expectations carry statistical risk, where a per-query certifier pays log M instead
of log K (Prop. 3). The output is an admission decision under an SLA. And it is now evaluated
as a system, on real indexes, with tail latency, throughput and queueing.

NOVELTY / SYSTEMS CONTRIBUTION (R1-W1). Fair reading of a submission whose only systems
measurement was an overhead micro-benchmark. The contribution is a query-optimizer design
point -- binding a physical plan at workload-window granularity and making that binding
auditable before it serves traffic. The unit choice is what makes the guarantee affordable
(above); the mechanism is a compiler decision (abstain to a feasibility-best fallback in plan
space), not a post-hoc recalibration; and the payoff is now measured in serving terms
(violations removed, +75% throughput, -28% p95, +18% capacity). The statistics are deliberately
textbook and we claim no new inequality.
