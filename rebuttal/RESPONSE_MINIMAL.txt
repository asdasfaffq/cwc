Two reviewer findings changed the paper's content; we lead with them.

R2, CODE. Correct: the released compiler let the calibration split both choose and certify the
deployed plan. Auditing the rest of the data flow we found a second instance no review raised
(the fallback was also screened on calibration data) and report it here. Both fixed. On 75
blocks with identical splits, the repair matching Prop. 2 verbatim leaves the certified bound
NUMERICALLY IDENTICAL (0.2629 at K=4/80 ms) and lowers realized violation (0.0014 -> 0.0011);
a union-bound alternative costs +0.033 and nothing else. Bound >= realized in 225/225 runs.

R2-W1/D1, GAMMA. We no longer estimate it. CWC is transductive, so on any operator-declared
coarsening both window marginals are observable and Gamma_c = max_z Q_c(z)/P_c(z) is COMPUTED.
The remaining assumption (within-stratum exchangeability) is testable and passes: train-vs-eval
conditional-loss gap 0.0012 mean, 0.027 max. Cost: 0.298 -> 0.313 bound width. It also shows
our plug-in was NOT conservative (needed kappa*=3.95) -- reported against ourselves. Recipe:
declare the coarsest partition you accept, compute Gamma from the observed marginals, run the
gap test on historical windows, refine until it passes.

R1-W2 / R2-D4 / R3-D2, END-TO-END. New harness, all real: sparse-matrix BM25, hnswlib HNSW over
E5-base, RRF, MiniLM cross-encoder, metadata predicate placed PRE (constrained ANN traversal)
or POST, over a 100,785-document union of five BEIR collections. No cached rows; CWC pays its
probe and routing inside the measured path. Pre-filter cost spans 0.48 ms (selectivity 1.0) to
78.6 ms (0.02), so placement is a real decision. At a 100 ms SLO, 5 seeds: CWC cuts the
strongest calibrated static plan's realized violation 30x (20.4% -> 0.7%), with +75% throughput
(27.1 vs 15.4 qps) and -28% p95 (82.1 vs 114.4 ms), at 3.9% nDCG cost -- matching the ~4% the
paper claimed from cached rows. Open-loop: CWC's capacity is 21.2 qps vs 18.0 and 12.2, and its
response-p95 advantage widens with load (175 vs 226, 219 vs 450, 429 vs 1074 ms). Boundary we
state rather than hide: at 10-25 ms SLOs one cheap plan is optimal everywhere, so the probe is
pure overhead (0.0953 vs 0.0957 nDCG at half the throughput).

R2-W2/D2, ADMISSION. Redone so the DECISION uses only compile-time information, and the
certificate is granted to EVERY deployment (it is a property of the analysis, not of CWC),
separating the rule from the policy. 30 blocks, tau=0.05: under the certificate rule all seven
deployments breach 0% of admitted windows; the best certificate-free prospective signal
breaches 13.0%, a point estimate 33.3%. CWC passes its own certificate on 96.7% of windows vs
26.7-33.3% for calibrated static plans, earning 2.5x the utility (0.496 vs 0.200) and beating
the clairvoyant post-hoc rule applied to that baseline (0.400).

R2-W3/D3, TIGHTNESS. R_cert has a floor Gamma*L_hat plus a 1/sqrt(n) term. Sweeping window
size, n_c 68 -> 688 gives R_cert 0.368 -> 0.037, valid throughout: tau=0.20 at n_c=121,
tau=0.10 at n_c=341, tau=0.05 at n_c=688 (R_cert 0.0435, realized 0.0031). tau=0.01 is
sample-size-bound (floor 0.0015), needing n_c ~ 12k. The 0.30 in the submission was the small
3-collection regime, not a property of the bound.

R3-D1, MOTIVATION. Measured: as the mix drifts, a calibrated fixed plan's violation rises

x1.08. Safe fixed recipes give up 7-20 nDCG points (0.533 / 0.407 vs 0.604).

R1-W1 + RELEVANCE. The object is physical-plan selection under a resource constraint; the unit
of decision is the workload, as in physical database design, and that choice is what makes the

where a per-query certifier pays log M instead of log K, Prop. 3); the output is an SLA
admission decision; and it is now evaluated as a system, with tail latency, throughput and
queueing. We claim the design point and the system, not a new inequality.
